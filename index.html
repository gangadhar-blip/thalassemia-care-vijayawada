MANAGEMENT

Thalassemia Treatment & Management

Thalassemia management is individualized according to the patient's condition, transfusion requirements, iron status and specialist assessment.

🩸

Blood Transfusion

Regular red blood cell transfusions may be required for people with transfusion-dependent Thalassemia. Transfusion plans are determined by the treating medical team.

💊

Iron Chelation

Repeated transfusions can lead to iron accumulation. Iron chelation medicines may be prescribed to help reduce excess iron.

🔬

Regular Monitoring

Ongoing monitoring may include blood counts, iron studies and assessment of organs affected by iron overload.

🏥

Care Coordination

Coordination between patients, families, doctors, day-care teams and blood-bank services is an important part of long-term care.

PREVENTION

Preventing Thalassemia in Future Generations

Awareness, carrier screening and appropriate genetic counselling can help families understand Thalassemia inheritance and available reproductive options.

🧬

Carrier Screening

Screening can identify people who carry a Thalassemia-related gene variant, even when they do not have significant symptoms.

👨‍👩‍👧

Genetic Counselling

Genetic counselling can help individuals and couples understand inheritance patterns and discuss reproductive choices.

🔬

Prenatal Diagnosis

Where appropriate, specialist-led prenatal testing can help determine whether a pregnancy is affected by a genetic condition.

🌱

Reproductive Options

Depending on the family's circumstances, specialist genetic and fertility teams can discuss available reproductive options.

CURATIVE OPTIONS

Hematopoietic Stem-Cell Transplantation

Hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation, commonly referred to as bone marrow transplantation (BMT), can provide a potential curative treatment for selected patients with Thalassemia.

Suitability depends on multiple factors including the patient's age, overall health, disease status and availability of an appropriate donor.

Patients should be assessed by an experienced specialist transplant team to understand potential benefits, risks and alternatives.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

General information about Thalassemia and its management.

What is Thalassemia? +

Thalassemia is an inherited blood disorder that affects the body's ability to produce normal haemoglobin. The severity and treatment requirements vary between individuals.

Does every person with Thalassemia need blood transfusions? +

No. Treatment requirements depend on the type and severity of Thalassemia. People with transfusion- dependent disease may require regular transfusions, while others may not.

What is a Thalassemia carrier? +

A carrier has a genetic change associated with Thalassemia but generally does not have the severe form of the disease. Carrier status can be identified through appropriate blood and genetic testing.

Can Thalassemia be cured? +

Hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation can be potentially curative for selected patients. Eligibility and suitability must be assessed by a specialist transplant team.

Why is iron chelation important? +

Repeated blood transfusions can cause iron to build up in the body. Excess iron can affect organs, so appropriate monitoring and chelation therapy may be recommended by the treating team.
